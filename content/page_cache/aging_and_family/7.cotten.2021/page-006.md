The digital divide
    Though it may be narrowing, a digital divide persists,
with individuals living in rural areas, having lower
income, and being older in age (e.g., 65 and older) being
less likely to have access or use technology (see
Fig. 23.5; Anderson & Kumar, 2019). Cost is an impor-
tant aspect of the digital divide for older adults, as is
cognitive ability—particularly for those aged 80 and old-
er. Cognitive decline negatively affects older adults, in
particular, and their ability to learn to use and/or con-
tinue to use ICTs over time (Cotten et al., 2017).
    Socioeconomic status has also been associated with
whether individuals have access to and use ICTs, as
well as how individuals use them (Anderson & Perrin,
2017; Cotten, Francis, Kadylak, et al., 2016; Hargittai &
Dobransky, 2017; Smith et al., 2017), with those with
lower levels of education and income typically having
less access to ICTs as well as lower levels of proficiency
with using ICTs. Findings by Pew (Anderson & Perrin,
