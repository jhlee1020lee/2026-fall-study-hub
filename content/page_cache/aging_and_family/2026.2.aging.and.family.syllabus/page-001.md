COURSE DESCRIPTION:
This course provides a deeper understanding of population aging and the challenges and
opportunities adults face at the individual, familial, and societal level due to a prolonged
adulthood. The topics include changing views of aging, social ties in later life,
intergenerational relationships in aging families, work and retirement, family caregiving and
support services for older adults, aging in place, death and bereavement, and social policy
issues. During the semester, we will deconstruct myths of aging and stereotypes about older
adults, examine the roles we have constructed for older people in society, and analyze the
impact of gender and social class on the aging process. One major objective of the course is
to have students engage actively and critically with the material. This objective is
accomplished through preparation and class discussions.


PREREQUISITES:
This course is an advanced course of aging and gerontology. Students must have successfully
completed “Human Development” (M2808.000900) OR “Adult Development and Aging”
(M2808.002900) for admission to the course.
