Abstract
Objectives: Being and feeling generative, defined as exhibiting concern and behavior to benefit others, is an important
developmental goal of midlife and beyond. Although a growing body of evidence suggests mental and physical health ben-
efits of feeling generative in later life, little information exists as to the modifiability of generativity perceptions. The present
study examines whether participation in the intergenerational civic engagement program, Experience Corps (EC), benefits
older adults’ self-perceptions of generativity.
Method: Levels of generativity were compared in older adults randomized to serve as EC volunteers or controls (usual
volunteer opportunities) in the Baltimore Experience Corps Trial at 4-, 12-, and 24-month evaluation points over the 2-year
trial. Analyses utilized intention-to-treat and complier average causal effects (CACE) analyses which incorporate degree of
intervention exposure in analytic models.
Results: Participants randomized to the EC group had significantly higher levels of generative desire and perceptions of
generative achievement than controls at each follow-up point; CACE analyses indicate a dose–response effect with a greater
magnitude of intervention effect with greater exposure to the EC program.
Discussion: Results provide the first-ever, large-scale experimental demonstration that participation in an intergenerational
civic engagement program can positively alter self-perceptions of generativity in older adulthood.
Keywords: Civic engagement—Generativity—Intergenerational—Randomized controlled trial—Social engagement—Volunteerism
