 Well-Ordering Property
• Well-ordering property: Every nonempty set of positive integers has a least element. In
  other words, for every 𝑆 ⊆ ℕ such that 𝑆 ≠ ∅, there exists 𝑚 ∈ 𝑆 such that 𝑚 ≤ 𝑛 for all
  𝑛 ∈ 𝑆.
  – The well-ordering property is one of the axioms of ℕ, that is, a property that is not
   proved but is assumed to be true.
  – The well-ordering property can be used directly in proofs (e.g. The existence and
   uniqueness of Euclidean division).
  – The validity of the mathematical induction principle can be derived from the well
   ordering property.
